Analytical framework
====================

In the following, the analytical framework on the assessment of exposure, priority areas, and the potential benefits of nature-based solutions (NBS) is presented.

Pluvial flooding
----------------

- **Exposure**: Population exposed to flooding, as a function of population and annual flood probability estimated at the neighbourhood level, i.e., the hexagonal tiling.
- **Priority areas**: Using results about exposure to flood hazard distribution and a simple hydrological model. This model generates a series of watersheds, at the neighbourhood level, to account for downstream effects of runoff reduction.
- **NBS Protection benefits**: Describe the potential of existing green spaces to mitigate downstream flood exposure through runoff reduction.
- **NBS Creation benefits**: Describe the potential benefits derived from the implementation of NBS to reduce downstream flood exposure through runoff reduction by (i) changes in runoff resulting from an alteration of land cover following an assumed decrease of bare soil based on a curve number approach; and (ii) an increase of water storage capacity (for bioretention areas, open green spaces, and inland wetlands).


Heat stress mitigation
----------------------

- **Exposure**: Population potentially exposed to heat stress, determined as a function of population and the estimated annual average air temperature derived from applying a geographically-weighted and climate space-weighted regression model by Hooker, Duveiller, and Cescatti (2018).
- **Priority areas**: Higher priority corresponds to higher exposure.
- **NBS Protection benefits**: Describe heat stress benefits for populations within approximatey 100 m, with trees typically providing higher cooling compared to other types of vegetation. Protection benefits are thus derived as a function of tree cover, weighted by the population within the affected area.
- **NBS Creation benefits**: Describe estimated cooling impacts of NBS families, achieved through changes in land cover composition, primarily as a function of increasing tree cover, weighted by population. Cooling potential is derived from a Spatial Durbin Error regression model, thus considering both direct and indirect effects of land cover composition on air temperature.



Health and recreation
----------------------

- **Exposure**: Population without access to min. 1.0 ha of green space within 300 m.
- **Priority areas**: Higher priority corresponds to higher exposure.
- **NBS Protection benefits**: Health and recreation protection benefits are estimated as the sum of the population within 300 m of each green space pixel that is serviced by protection opportunities of min. 1.0 ha.
- **NBS Creation benefits**: Creation benefits are estimated by calculating the number of residents located within 300 m of contiguous NBS larger than 1.0 ha who would gain new access to a green space, having previously lacked access, weighted by recreational potential of the given NBS family.

Social cohesion
----------------------

- **Exposure**: Population without access to min. 0.5 ha of green space within 300 m.
- **Priority areas**: Higher priority corresponds to higher exposure.
- **NBS Protection benefits**: Social cohesion protection benefits are estimated as the sum of the population within 300 m of each green space pixel that is serviced by protection opportunities of min. 0.5 ha.
- **NBS Creation benefits**: Creation benefits are estimated by calculating the number of residents located within 300 m of contiguous NBS larger than 0.5 ha who would gain new access to a green space, having previously lacked access, weighted by recreational potential of the given NBS family.

Biodiversity
----------------------

- **Exposure**: Not applicable.
- **Priority areas**: Not applicable.
- **NBS Protection benefits**: Describe the potential of habitat patches to support biodiversity, estimated as a function of habitat quality based on the InVEST Habitat Quality Model. Higher habitat quality is assumed to provide higher benefits for biodiversity.
- **NBS Creation benefits**: Describe the change in structural connectivity of habitat patches assuming a connectivity threshold of (by default) 100 m and considering major roads to impede connectivity unless treed, based on adapted effective mesh size weighted by patch volumetric capacity.  

Erosion
----------------------

- **Exposure**: Population in erosion-prone areas.
- **Priority areas**: Higher priority corresponds to higher exposure.
- **NBS Protection benefits**: Describe the potential of existing green spaces to mitigate soil erosion (RUSLE model, Karydas & Panagos, 2018).
- **NBS Creation benefits**: Describe the potential of NBS to reduce soil erosion by combining spatial estimates of annual soil erosion rates with NBS-specific weighting factors that represent the relative effectiveness of each NBS family to mitigate erosion.

Jobs creation
----------------------

- **Exposure**: Not applicable.
- **Priority areas**: Not applicable.
- **NBS Protection benefits**: Not applicable.
- **NBS Creation benefits**: Highlights areas where people are likely to be employed through NBS initiatives, as a function of accessibility and the potential capacity of a specific NBS family to provide employment and resource production benefits.
